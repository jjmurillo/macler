In gradient descent the step is proportional to the norm of the gradient. In RMSProp and Adam each component is divided by an average of its magnitude, and the step depends mostly on α.

The iterates are computed in the browser with a fixed α, stopping when ‖w(t+1) − w(t)‖ < 10−4. The optimizers in the comparison follow the PyTorch update rules (SGD with momentum and Nesterov, RMSprop and Adam), and their trajectories match torch.optim to machine precision; in the comparison there is no stopping rule, but a common horizon of K steps. The example function is adapted from F. Fleuret, The Little Book of Deep Learning (2023), Fig. 3.2, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. “Curved valley” and “Narrow valley” are original example functions. Keyboard: space plays or pauses, ← → move one step, Home and End jump to the ends.
