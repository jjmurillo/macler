Logistic regression with one feature

Each sample has one feature, x, and a label y ∈ {0, 1}. The model gives the probability that y = 1, and we decide class 1 when that probability is at least 0.5. Move the weights, change the data, and run gradient descent to see where the loss comes from and how the weights are learned.

Loss of one sample. ℓi = −[yi log hi + (1−yi) log(1−hi)], that is, −log hi for a 1 and −log(1−hi) for a 0.

A sample on the right side of the boundary, with a confident output, adds almost nothing. A sample on the wrong side adds a lot, and the further it is, the more: the loss has no upper bound.

J(w) = (1/m) Σ ℓi is the average over the m samples (binary cross-entropy).

With least squares, (14, 1) pulls the line and shifts its threshold even though it is far from the classes' border. In logistic regression, once it is well classified its loss is close to 0.

How gradient descent learns the weights

Gradient. ∇J = (1/m) Σ (hi − yi) (1, xi). Each sample pulls with its error hi − yi, between −1 and 1, times (1, xi).

Update. w := w − α∇J, the arrow on the map. Too large an α overshoots the valley and J goes up.

Standardizing x. With raw x (up to 14), J changes much faster along w₁ than along w₀, so the valley is long and narrow. Standardized, it is rounder and a larger α works: notebook Block II.12 uses α = 0.6 raw and α = 10 standardized.
